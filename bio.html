About

Earth observation data is only valuable when the people who need it can actually use it. That is the problem I have spent a decade on — in refugee camps, in policy briefings, and now on satellite intelligence platforms.

I am a Solutions Architect at Ursa Space Systems, a commercial satellite tasking company. On the object analytics requirements team I work at the meeting point of SAR imagery, AI pipelines, and geospatial intelligence delivery, defining requirements and architecting capabilities across an intelligence-gathering stack of more than ninety satellite and detection analytic providers, including Sentinel-1, Capella, ICEYE and Umbra. I co-developed the platform curriculum that trains our intelligence and operations teams, and contribute to the target taxonomy and fine-tuning pipeline behind how our AI agent reasons about what it observes.

My route here was not a straight line. I hold a PhD in Global Governance and Human Security from the University of Massachusetts Boston, specializing in decision science and GIS. Before commercial satellite intelligence I was an inaugural Science for Development Fellow at USAID, serving as an Energy Technical Adviser and sitting on the U.S. Group on Earth Observations advisory committee. Prior to that I was International Data Scientist with Global IDEAS at Michigan State University, where I built a decision platform for tracking international development funding activity — enough of a going concern that peer universities asked about adopting it — and wrote the standard operating procedures governing how the university pursues international funding and handles research, contracts and grants in that domain. Earlier I worked in the UN system as a Project Support Officer with UNRWA’s West Bank Field Office, and as a post-conflict reconstruction researcher in Liberia.

That mix — field work, policy, and remote sensing — is why I care less about imagery for its own sake and more about whether the decision downstream of it is any good.

What I work on

  • Satellite tasking and SAR. Getting the right sensor pointed at the right place at the right time, and making the resulting data usable.
  • Geospatial AI. Change detection, classification, target taxonomy, and agentic pipelines for Earth observation workflows.
  • Participatory and volunteered geographic information. Co-founder of the International Society for Participatory Mapping, and a decade of research and practice on how communities produce spatial data, and what happens when that data meets institutional decision-making. More on that work here.

Writing

I edit and contribute to academic volumes on geospatial methods, most recently Global Land-Ocean Geospatial Applications (CRC Press, 2025) and Evaluating Participatory Mapping Software (Springer, 2023). A full list is on the books and publications pages.

Earlier work on social movements and social media was cited in the 2023 Economic Report of the President, the Council of Economic Advisers’ annual report to Congress.
