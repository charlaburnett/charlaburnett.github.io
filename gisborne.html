Gisborne Reforestation Change Detection

Radar sees through cloud, which is most of what sits over the East Cape of New Zealand’s North Island. This project uses SAR backscatter to track how forest cover across the Gisborne District changed between 2020 and 2023, separating land that gained woody structure from land that lost it.

Method

  • Two SAR epochs. Backscatter composites for 2020 and 2023 over the Gisborne District, assembled in Google Earth Engine.
  • Differencing. The two epochs are differenced into a backscatter change layer — rising backscatter indicating a gain in woody structure, falling backscatter indicating removal.
  • Classification. That change layer is thresholded into two classes, likely reforestation and likely clearing, shown above in green and red.
  • Delivery. Published as an interactive map with before, after, raw change and classified layers each independently toggleable over an OpenStreetMap basemap, so any two can be compared directly.

Why radar

Optical change detection is difficult on this coast because persistent cloud leaves the usable optical record thin and irregularly spaced. SAR is unaffected by cloud and acquires on a consistent repeat cycle, which makes a clean two-epoch comparison possible where an optical time series would have gaps in exactly the seasons that matter.

Status

The map above is a capture of the working version. The live map is served from Earth Engine tile endpoints whose access tokens have since expired, so it is being rebuilt against durable exported layers before it goes up here.
