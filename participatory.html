Participatory Mapping & Volunteered GIS

Most of my published research is about a single question: what happens when the people who live somewhere are the ones producing the spatial data about it, and that data then has to survive contact with institutional decision-making.

The short answer is that the software usually gets in the way. Participatory GIS tools are built by people who understand geospatial data and used by people who understand a place, and the gap between those two groups is where most participatory mapping projects quietly fail. That gap is what I have spent a decade documenting, evaluating, and occasionally building around.

That work has run through communities in the United States, Antigua and Barbuda, Honduras, Liberia, Rwanda, Uganda, Malawi, Brazil, Mexico, Colombia and Palestine — building participatory mapping tools with migrants, internally displaced people and indigenous populations, and conducting field interviews for evaluations including UNIDO’s.

Research

Evaluating Participatory Mapping Software

Springer, 2023. An edited volume comparing participatory mapping platforms against the needs of the communities and practitioners actually using them, with contributions from practitioners across several continents. It remains my most-cited book-length work.

Lessons learned from SeaSketch

“Incorporating the participatory process in the design of geospatial support tools: Lessons learned from SeaSketch,” Environmental Modelling & Software 127 (2020), 104678. A study of what design decisions in a marine spatial planning tool do to the participatory process running through it.

Blue Halo — Antigua and Barbuda, Montserrat

As a Spatial Planning Fellow with UC Santa Barbara’s Marine Science Institute (2017–2019), I determined the geodesign process model used by SeaSketch and built an evaluation framework for the Blue Halo marine spatial planning project, funded by the Waitt Institute. That framework was subsequently used by more than twenty-seven research scientists, software developers and practitioners in the collaboratively edited volume below.

Doctoral research

“Evaluating perceptions of participatory mapping for public decision-making using SeaSketch,” University of Massachusetts Boston. My dissertation, extending the SeaSketch work to how participants themselves perceive legitimacy and influence in mapping-supported public decisions.

Service and community practice

The research came out of practice, not the other way around.

  • International Society for Participatory Mapping — co-founder, and Secretarial Officer on the executive board. ISPM was created to give participatory mapping a professional home of its own, bringing together practitioners from the Amazon Conservation Team, Mapbox, National Geographic Maps and universities across four continents around a shared standard for what meaningful public participation in mapping actually requires. I ran the financial aid programme for the society’s 2019 conference, assessing applications and chairing the award decisions across the executive committee, and recruited and built out its editorial board.
  • UN Maps Learning Hub — OpenStreetMap mapping and validation training through the United Nations open geospatial data programme, which collects and validates map data for the areas where UN field operations work (2025).
  • GISCorps (volunteer, 2022–2024) — the URISA programme that places vetted GIS professionals on humanitarian and disaster-response projects. I served on the PhotoMappers team, which the National Alliance for Public Safety GIS Foundation activates to triage aerial imagery during hurricanes, floods and tornado outbreaks.
  • Code for Lansing (2019) — a Code for America brigade I helped start. I opened the brigade’s website repository with its initial commit in August 2019 and built the site out over the following weeks, adapting it from the Lansing Codes community site; I am still its second-highest contributor. The brigade went on to run civic projects for the Lansing area, and I have since contributed through Code for Boston as well.
  • Refugees Welcome! — co-founder.
  • Umoja House (founder, 2021–2024) — a temporary home in Lansing, Michigan for migrants of all kinds: international scholars, refugees, asylees, and internally displaced people.
  • OrganizingTogether (senior data analyst, 2019–2023) — a digital laboratory for citizen science, using participatory communication and research methods to build working relationships between decision-makers, scientists and citizens, with GIS running through the planning and evaluation work.
